# Supplier Terms

Harrow & Finch Gifts Ltd (fictional). Purchasing and supplier terms, version 2.0, effective 1 March 2011. Synthetic document written for the RetailOps Agent project.

## 1. Purchase orders

### 1.1 Who may raise a purchase order
A purchase order may be drafted by any member of the operations team or by an automated system, but it becomes binding only once it has been approved by a named person with purchasing authority. Drafted orders that haven't been approved are not sent to suppliers. Every approval is recorded with the approver's name and a timestamp.

### 1.2 Approval limits
Operations staff may approve purchase orders up to £5,000 in value. Orders above £5,000 also need sign-off from a director. A single stock code may not be split across several purchase orders to stay under this limit.

### 1.3 Order quantities and minimum order quantities
Suppliers sell in cartons. The minimum order quantity (MOQ) is one carton, which is 12 units unless the product listing says otherwise. Order quantities must be rounded up to a whole number of cartons.

### 1.4 Purchase order contents
Each purchase order must state the stock code, quantity, agreed unit cost, supplier ID, requested delivery date and the reason for the order (for example "below reorder point" or "seasonal build").

## 2. Reordering rules

### 2.1 When to reorder
A stock code should be reordered when its on-hand quantity is at or below its reorder point. The reorder point is the expected demand over the supplier lead time plus one week of safety stock.

### 2.2 How much to order
The recommended order quantity is the forecast demand over the lead time plus two weeks, minus the current on-hand quantity, rounded up to a whole carton. Where a demand forecast with an interval is available, planners should use the median forecast for routine items and the upper (90th percentile) forecast for items with high stock-out cost, such as best-sellers in the Christmas season.

### 2.3 Slow-moving items
A slow-moving item is one whose unit sales in the last quarter were at least 30% lower than in the quarter before. Slow movers should not normally be reordered. They may be reordered anyway if the demand forecast for the next four weeks exceeds the on-hand stock, or if the item is a seasonal line entering its peak season, and the reason must be written on the purchase order.

### 2.4 Discontinued items
Items that have had no sales for 90 days are treated as discontinued and must not be reordered without a director's approval.

### 2.5 Seasonal cut-off dates
To arrive in time for Christmas trading, purchase orders for Christmas lines must be approved by 15 October. Orders approved after that date are accepted only if the supplier confirms delivery before 1 December.

## 3. Lead times and delivery

### 3.1 Standard lead times
Each supplier has an agreed lead time of 7, 14, 21 or 28 days from purchase order approval to delivery at our warehouse. The lead time for each stock code is held in the stock levels table.

### 3.2 Late delivery
If a delivery is more than seven days late, we may cancel the undelivered part of the purchase order without charge. Repeated late deliveries are reviewed at the quarterly supplier meeting.

### 3.3 Delivery inspection
Goods-in staff check every delivery against the purchase order. Shortages, damage and incorrect items must be reported to the supplier within five working days of delivery.

## 4. Supplier returns and credits

### 4.1 Faulty stock
Faulty stock can be returned to the supplier for full credit within 60 days of delivery to our warehouse. The supplier pays return carriage for faulty stock.

### 4.2 Overstock returns
Suppliers are not obliged to accept overstock returns. Suppliers S001 to S004 have agreed to accept overstock returns of up to 10% of the quantity bought in the previous 12 months, subject to a 15% restocking fee. Other suppliers must be asked case by case.

### 4.3 Customer returns of supplier-faulty items
When a customer returns an item as faulty and the fault is a manufacturing defect, the item is logged and returned to the supplier under section 4.1. The 60-day window is counted from our goods-in date, not the customer's purchase date.

## 5. Payment terms

### 5.1 Standard terms
Supplier invoices are paid 30 days after the end of the month in which they are received (net 30 EOM).

### 5.2 Early payment discount
Where a supplier offers a 2% early payment discount for payment within 10 days, finance will take it if cash flow allows.

### 5.3 Disputed invoices
Invoices that don't match an approved purchase order and a goods-in record are put on hold until the difference is resolved. Payment terms start from the date the dispute is resolved.

## 6. Supplier conduct

### 6.1 Ethical sourcing
Suppliers must comply with the Modern Slavery Act 2015 and confirm this each year.

### 6.2 Product safety
Suppliers of toys and children's products must provide UKCA or CE conformity documents before the first delivery.
